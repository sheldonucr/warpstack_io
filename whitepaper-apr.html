White paper · No. 04

Resolution on demand

Method: numerical · detailed 3D Package: AMD MI350-class accelerator Hardware: one NVIDIA GB10 desktop Series: WarpStack white papers
Abstract

Warpage is a whole-package quantity, and it settles on a coarse mesh — white paper No. 01 shows it. Stress is not. It concentrates at die corners and gaps a few tens of microns across, and a coarse mesh averages it away. Resolution on demand lets you name the critical regions and the resolution you need there — 50 µm, 10 µm — and returns the package warpage and those regions' stresses together. On a production-class AMD MI350-class package it delivers 10 µm at five critical corners in 5.4 minutes on a single desktop, where a uniform 10 µm mesh is projected at 2.9 billion degrees of freedom and ~25 TB of memory. Warpage matches the converged reference to 0.2%; the peak corner stress rises 5.6× over what the coarse mesh reports.

Two answers, two resolutions

Warpage settles early. Stress does not.

A package bows over tens of millimetres; a die corner concentrates stress over tens of microns. One uniform mesh cannot serve both without becoming unaffordable. With resolution on demand you ask for exactly what each question needs: the package at the resolution where warpage has converged, and 50 µm or 10 µm at the corners and gaps you name — in one run, in minutes.

A uniform 50 µm mesh needs about a terabyte and three days; a uniform 10 µm mesh needs roughly 1,000× the memory this run used — some 200× what the desktop even holds — and about five months. Resolution on demand delivered both resolutions in minutes, in 27 GB. The uniform rows are projections, not results — they were never solved, because no single machine on hand could hold them.
What this buys you

One run, both answers. Package warpage at the resolution where it has converged, and the stress at every corner and gap you care about at the resolution you ask for — 50 µm, 10 µm — in minutes on a desktop. The corners that will crack first are seen at the scale they fail at, without paying for that scale across the whole 64 × 72 mm package.

Scope of this study. Same package model as white paper No. 01, solved with the current WarpStack release: a free package body with no board, BGA layer, lid or stiffener, no moisture, cure shrinkage or gravity, and library material properties rather than vendor data. Stresses are linear-elastic peak interface stresses for the 200 → 85 °C load. At a sharp corner where two materials meet, peak stress keeps rising as resolution increases; the compute-die corner value is a severity indicator to be read alongside your design rules, not a converged material stress. Uniform-mesh figures are projections. None of these numbers is a prediction of shipping hardware. AMD, Instinct and MI350 are trademarks of Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.
