WarpStack white papers

A growing series of short technical notes behind WarpStack. Each one takes a single method, states the package it was measured on, shows the tables and figures, and sets out the scope and caveats — so every number on the capabilities page can be read in full here.

No. 04
Fidelity · numerical

Resolution on demand — 10 µm where it matters

Warpage settles on a coarse mesh; corner stress does not. Name the critical regions and the resolution you need, and WarpStack returns package warpage and local stress together. On the AMD MI350-class package it delivers 10 µm at five critical corners in 5.4 minutes in 27 GB on one desktop — where a uniform 10 µm mesh is projected at 2.9 billion DOF and ~25 TB. Warpage lands within 0.2% of the converged reference, and the peak corner stress comes in 5.6× higher than the coarse mesh reports.

5.4 min at 10 µm 27 GB, not 25 TB AMD MI350-class

No. 03
Localized exploration · compact model

WarpStack-MOR — localized design-space exploration

A physics-based compact model built once over a bounded box around a nominal design — not a fit to data — carrying a rigorous error bound. On a what-if outside the box (ABF substrate stiffened 24.0→28.8 GPa, +40.9 µm of bow) it tracks the full 3D solve to within 0.036 µm while running in 51 µs — 798,000× faster. Includes the method agreement, the eight-point validation, and the cost model.

51 µs per design within 0.036 µm error bound attached

No. 02
Global exploration · AI

WarpStack-GNN — sweep the whole design space

A graph neural network trained on WarpStack's own 3D output returns the full warpage map in 19.4 ms — 2,146× faster than solving the same package in 3D, at 0.49% whole-map difference against independent 3D solves. Scored on 512 held-out material and thermal configurations it was never shown. Includes the accuracy tables, the per-design training cost, and the break-even point.

No. 01
Fidelity · numerical

High-fidelity at various resolution

Resolution is an input, not a result. On a production-class AMD MI350-class accelerator package, peak-to-peak warpage moves 0.33% across a 2.25× refinement range and the whole deflected field is unchanged to the width of a plotted line — so a resolution that runs in 11 minutes on a laptop already carries the answer a ~0.9 TB, day-long solve would return. Includes the convergence evidence, the surface-by-surface agreement, and the projected cost of the fine mesh we did not need to solve.

±0.55% total uncertainty 1.43 M DOF AMD MI350-class

WarpStack is a warpage engine for multi-layer 2.5D/3D chiplet, module, and package stacks. Speed and method-agreement figures (WarpStack-MOR and WarpStack-GNN) are measured on a 2nm CoWoS reference design — 8 HBM stacks × 12-hi DRAM + 2 GPUs, 108 chiplet instances — at FEM mesh 64, single core. Mechanical properties for that design are illustrative, not calibrated process data, so its absolute warpage in microns is indicative; timings and method-to-method agreement are not affected. Resolution and accuracy figures come from a separate study of an AMD MI350-class package, presented with its full scope and caveats in the white papers.
